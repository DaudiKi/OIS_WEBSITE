Billing lifecycle

From package to paid

The administrator only ever does one thing: decide what a pupil is getting. Everything downstream — the itemised statement, the portal access, the club register, the parent's balance — follows from that automatically.

STEP 1

Assemble

Admin picks items for the pupil — 12 PACEs, swimming, one e-learning module.

STEP 2

Enrol

One enrolment per item, each freezing today's price.

STEP 3

Draft statement

Lines regenerate freely while the invoice is a draft.

STEP 4

Issue

Lines freeze. From here corrections are credit notes.

STEP 5

Provision

Portal access granted; club registers updated.

Steps 3–5 are database triggers, not screens anybody visits. The only human action is step 1 — which is why the package builder is the one screen that has to be genuinely good.

Invoice states

draft issued part paid paid void — cancels the whole document; never used to fix one line

While it is a draft

Lines are a pure function of the pupil's enrolments. Add swimming, the line appears. Remove it, the line vanishes. Nothing to reconcile, because nothing has been promised to anyone yet.

Once it is issued

The parent has seen a number. Lines become immutable; withdrawing a pupil from swimming raises a credit note instead of quietly rewriting history. This is the single rule that keeps the fee ledger auditable.

Inside the fees document, and outside it

One flag on the catalogue item — billing_group — decides which section of the statement a line lands in. It is one statement either way, so a parent sees a single balance; the split is presentational and by section total, not two separate debts.

core — school fees proper billing_group = core
Tuition · PACE materials · lunch · transport · examination fees

Charged to every pupil as a matter of course. Appears in the top section, and is what "school fees" means in conversation with a parent.

extra — chosen, not assumed billing_group = extra
Clubs · swimming · e-learning modules · training · event tickets

Opted into for this pupil, this term. Listed separately so a parent can see exactly what the optional part of the bill is — and drop it next term without touching the core.

Eight things to settle before anyone writes code

The first three are not opinions — getting them wrong corrupts the fee ledger in ways that are painful to unwind. The rest are genuine policy choices that only the school can make.

01

Freeze the price at assignment

An invoice line must carry the price it was sold at, not a pointer to today's catalogue price. Otherwise raising swimming fees next term silently rewrites last term's issued invoices — and every reconciliation after it.

02

Money is an integer

Store UGX as bigint, never a float. Floating point cannot represent money exactly, and the error compounds across hundreds of pupils until a termly total is visibly wrong by a few shillings that nobody can account for.

03

Parents must be linkable to children first

This is already the system's biggest gap, and billing makes it fatal rather than merely awkward: a parent who cannot be linked to a pupil cannot be shown an invoice at all. Fix that before building any of this.

04

Does non-payment close the portal?

If an e-learning module is unpaid, does the pupil lose access mid-term? Cutting a child off from schoolwork over a parent's arrears is a policy question, not an engineering one — but the answer changes what provisioning has to watch.

05

Who may add an extra?

If a parent can sign their child up for swimming from the portal, they are creating a charge on their own bill. That is convenient and it is also a commitment — decide whether it needs an administrator to confirm before it becomes billable.

06

Consent is not payment

Swimming and off-site trips need a parent's permission as well as their money. Keep the two states separate: paid-but-unconsented and consented-but-unpaid are both real, and both must block the child going in the water.

07

Club capacity belongs in the database

Twenty places and two parents signing up at once will both pass an application-level check. Enforce the limit with a constraint, and decide now whether the loser is refused or waitlisted.

08

A secret ballot needs two tables

Record that a pupil voted separately from what the vote was. Put both in one row and any administrator can see how each child voted for their own student council — which is exactly what a ballot is supposed to prevent.
