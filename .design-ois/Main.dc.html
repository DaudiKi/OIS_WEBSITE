Refining what's already there

The blue and green stay — parents recognise them. What changes is discipline: the gradient retires, the two brand colours get distinct jobs, and type gets a real hierarchy. Six decisions, each with a reason.

01

The gradient retires

The blue-to-green gradient appears in more than fifteen places across every stylesheet — page headers, buttons, cards, dividers. It is the single most dating element in the current design, and it makes text sitting on it hard to read. Flat colour replaces it everywhere.

02

Blue and green get separate jobs

Today the two brand colours are used interchangeably, so neither means anything. From here: blue is interaction — links, buttons, focus, anything you can click. Green is affirmation — published, present, accepted, passed. Colour starts carrying information.

OIS Green — affirmation #4e9236 · fills & icons
Green, darkened for text #35661F · see decision 03
03

The bright green fails contrast — so it never carries text

This one is a genuine accessibility fault, not a taste call. #63b647 on white measures about 2.4:1 — well under the 4.5:1 minimum for body text. It stays for fills and icons; a darkened green takes over wherever green becomes words.

04

A display face with a voice

Righteous is a novelty face doing serious work — it appears on page headings across the site. Newsreader replaces it for headings: warm, editorial, and readable at every size. Figtree carries body and interface text, with tabular figures for the AMS.

The OIS wordmark itself is untouched — that is a brand asset, not interface type.

05

Status colour, separate from brand colour

The report workflow has five states and today they are told apart mostly by reading. Each gets a fixed colour and a fixed shape, used identically everywhere it appears — dashboard, list, editor.

06

One identity, two densities

The public site breathes — generous margins, large type, room around images. The AMS is a tool people use all day, so it tightens: 36px controls, 14px data type, more rows on screen. Same palette, same faces, different rhythm.
